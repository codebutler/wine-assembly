## Read this before the table

Everything below this heading down to "Repro command lines" is generated by
`tools/block-exec-sweep.js`. This section is not, and it is what the numbers
mean.

**All 14 CRASH rows crash with the executor OFF as well.** Every one is
`BOTH` arms, at the same batch, with the same message — eleven trap at batch 0
(`wordpad`, `hypertrm`, `telnet`, `mspaint_ep`, `tworld`, `scr_corbis`,
`scr_fashion`, `scr_horror`, `scr_win98`, `scr_wotravel`) or in a `DllMain`
(`kodakimg`, `warcraft3_demo`), and `pinball` at batch 23 and `ut2004_demo` at
batch 220 do so identically in both arms. **`--block-exec` introduced no crash
anywhere in the registry.** These are the corpus's existing unimplemented-API
gaps; they are in the table because the sweep cannot photograph an app that
traps, not because the executor did anything to them.

No on-vs-off number from a NONDET app is evidence about anything. **Always
pass `--control` when a row is going to be quoted**; the flag exists because
this sweep was run without it first and produced thirteen rows that looked like
executor differences and were not.

**The five survivors are all inside their own null band.** Three
(`broken_sword_demo` 3.00% against a 27.11% off-vs-off band, `abedemo` 1.28%
against 5.14%, `dx_foxbear` 0.023% against 47.65%) are an order of magnitude
under the band and are classed DIFFERENT only because they are identical at 300
and differ at 600, which fails the "converge" half of the PACING rule. The
other two sit *at* it: `quake2_demo` 0.401% against a 0.401% band and
`dx_flip2d` 16.337% against 16.296%. Not one of the 201 apps produced an
on-vs-off difference that is large against how much that same app moves between
two budgets on its own.

**A caveat on the null band, from the round-14 measurement next door.** The
band used here is off@300 vs off@600. On a title whose animation has already
*settled* by 300 batches that band is 0 or near it, so a genuine fraction-of-a-
frame phase difference has nothing to be measured against and reads as
DIFFERENT. Heroes II is the worked example: two off runs at 1400 are
byte-identical, but one batch of guest time moves 1.43% of its pixels, and
`--no-block-exec-regions` reproduces the on-arm picture exactly. `--control`
catches the noisy half of this; the settled half needs a same-budget
neighbouring-batch comparison the tool does not do yet.

**Six DIFFERENT rows also had an arm run out of clock.** `dx_ddex4`,
`dx_ddex5`, `dx_twist`, `dx_flip3dtl`, `dx_wormhole` and `scr_cityscap` each
had at least one arm stop on `--max-seconds` short of 600 batches
(`scr_cityscap` worst: 131 batches off against 393 on), so those arms
photographed different moments for reasons unrelated to the executor. Box
`loadavg` ran **18.9 → 72.8** over the 2075s sweep with several other agent
sweeps live; the pixel comparisons are load-immune but *how far a time-capped
run gets* is not. The tool now records `reached` per run and says `time-capped`
in the note for exactly this.

**Five NOPIC rows never painted in any arm** (`snood`, `snood_installer`,
`baldurs_gate_noninteractive_demo`, `baldurs_gate_interactive_demo`,
`baldurs_gate_chapters_1_2_demo`). They are not a verdict about the executor —
they are apps that need more than 600 batches at this batch size to put
anything on screen.
