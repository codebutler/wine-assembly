**Why the two event orders can differ.** Candidate exports lines 4659–4676 calls `$wnd_send_message`; dialog runtime (historical artifact: `shadow-man-original-first-zero-cause-20261023/09c3a-dialog-runtime.wat`) lines 176–208 saves caller EIP/ESP, enters the synchronous child, emits sender-pre *before* building its frame, pushes `$sync_msg_ret_thunk` and enters the resolved WndProc. Candidate dispatch (historical artifact: `shadow-man-original-first-zero-cause-20261023/09b-dispatch.wat`) lines 1332–1337 implements `CACA0005` by explicitly setting EIP=0 to stop this nested run. Only after that does the sender normally emit before-restore and restore the outer caller. The armed observer instead exits the host process at that first zero; adapter lines 38–46 and preload line 32 explain OS exit 86 and why sender-before-restore is absent. Beenden dispatch (historical artifact: `shadow-man-original-first-zero-cause-20261023/original/original-evidence/beenden-dispatch.json`) consequently reports a socket hangup, not a completed guest callback result.

By contrast, `CACA0004` (dispatch lines 961–1059) owns modal frame completion: it consumes sharedEnded, destroys/restores the modal frame, emits consume and complete, restores EIP from `dlg_ret_addr`, and clears ended. `EndDialog` (USER source lines 1434–1460) records ended/result and defers teardown to this pump; it does not itself jump to `0x4015d4`. The original's ended/sharedEnded 1 with pump/return unchanged is consistent with a pending outer modal, whereas synthetic first-zero has pump/return/ended/sharedEnded all zero after its callback-created modal completed. The synthetic can complete a modal *inside* its synchronous callback before reaching its callback-return zero; that is not the original's already-active-modal arrangement.

This is the concrete causal limit on the required ordering: **if** original Beenden returns normally to `CACA0005`, its nested stop precedes resumption of the outer `CACA0004` and thus precedes that pump's return to `0x4015d4`. Requiring those outer events before that nested zero cannot establish original modal completion. The accepted synthetic path does not resolve this mismatch. The trace nevertheless does **not** show which native branch actually wrote the original zero, so the conditional cannot be promoted to a proven normal-return verdict.

**Proven versus unresolved.** There is a preexisting active modal, a concrete abandoned Config/OK history, and a selected synchronous send ending at an unproven zero before the captured modal frame was consumed. That is stronger than merely listing missing events. There is no evidence of observer event loss. A genuinely unexpected control transfer to zero, or an abandoned/stale modal frame contributing to it, remains possible: neither callback entry `$wp`, the zero-producing predecessor/marker, nor full modal-frame provenance is retained. Token/root/depth equality and balanced ESP do not identify the zero writer. Conversely, absence of the outer continuation before a synchronous stop is not proof that Beenden bypassed its intended guest handler or that the game can never complete the modal. No production repair is justified yet; no oracle was weakened.
